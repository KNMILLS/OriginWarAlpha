## What changed

Two primitive-based look corrections were rejected rather than expanded. Their wide split stance and flat face remained inconsistent with the painted target. The construction method then changed: an explicitly CC0 anatomical head by Dan Ulrich from Blender's official Human Base Meshes bundle replaces the faceplate. The fitted helmet, continuous garment, cuffs, guards, harness, boots and equipment remain editable production work. Source, selected-object license evidence and rejected iterations are retained with the officer project.

The bounded opening batch contains76 clips and660 direct Blender renders for pistol/suit and unarmed/suit. Coverage includes idle/start/walk/stop, pistol ready/aim/fire, generic work, injury/death and unarmed guard/melee states. The exact binding ledger reports supplied and missing states. Dedicated turn transitions, later weapons/armor, task-specific pickup/equip handling, machines and door transitions remain separate gaps.

A walking loop now travels half a cell in0.6seconds using36 poses at60Hz. A full cell takes1.2seconds plus authored start/settle clips. This deliberate shorter stride removes the earlier need for overstretched legs. Ordinary nonmovement presentation slots remain0.5seconds; tactical costs are unchanged. Source-projected foot residual and held-frame bounds are useful checks, distinct from visible native foot planting and owner approval.

Native illustration sources use a256px reference-cell camera. Runtime frames are rendered directly at192px per reference cell (288x336 canvas), preserving logical bounds and normalized foot anchors. No per-frame paint filter is applied. Lossless atlas trimming removes transparent margins, deduplicates identical pixels and preserves world-space placement; editable source can reproduce the full-resolution master configuration.

Only the selected candidate's atlas pages remain resident. The officer has an eight-page/128MiB ceiling; the active art-texture estimate has a192MiB ceiling. Bounds, source pixels, anchor reconstruction, hashes and declared page budget are validated before packaging. These are art-texture estimates, not total process/GPU allocation measurements.

## Presentation integrity

Actual accepted commands and own-HP outcomes select work/injury/death poses. Machine injury cannot cause an officer flinch. Officer injury/death events now identify their subject; damage, random decisions, tactical rules and saved-state schema are unchanged. Final-tick incoming combat follows the completed player translation; early multi-tick responses keep their order. Reaction poses use the scheduled consequence cue time. Death is saved immediately, further actions remain rejected and the bounded visible death pose precedes the ending screen.

Pause/focus freezes the presentation clock. Missing clips remain visible as missing/static coverage rather than silently borrowing another weapon. Source/contract tests and complete paired saved-state comparisons support these bounded claims; they do not certify human pacing, sound quality or artistic finish.
